About

Machine learning is mostly plumbing, and that's as true for a fine-tuned LLM as for a churn model. The model is the glamorous 20%; the rest is data that has to arrive on time, a pipeline that can't break at 3am, and a serving stack that still makes sense months later. That's the part I find interesting, and the part most people skip.

Right now that means fine-tuning LLMs, wiring agentic workflows, and building the retrieval that sits underneath them.

I spent three years in telecommunications engineering before I ever trained a model. It taught me to read the math instead of memorizing it. I know what a Fourier transform is doing, why a convolution helps. ML and AI engineering felt like a continuation, not a pivot.

Most of what I've built started as a question I couldn't answer from a lecture slide.

I'm finishing my degree at URJC, interning on Repsol's data team, and running Datalab, the association I founded because project-driven learning didn't exist yet.
